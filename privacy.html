Data Collection & Consent Statement

What this document is

Giacomo is a research prototype built for DSTF, not a certified or commercially deployed medical device. This statement explains what personal and medical information the system collects, why, who can see it, how it's protected, and what rights you have over your own data. You should read this before registering.

What we collect

Account information: your name and login credentials.

Medical information (optional but required to activate emergency features): blood type, known allergies, chronic conditions, current medications, organ donor status, and up to three emergency contacts (name and phone number).

Device information: your helmet's serial number, linked to your account once set (this cannot be changed afterward, to prevent a printed QR code from pointing to the wrong record).

Location and motion data, only while the rider dashboard is actively open in your browser: GPS coordinates, used to show your position on the safety dashboard and, if a crash is detected, to relay your location to your emergency contacts and to anyone who scans your helmet's QR code afterward.

Incident records: if a crash is detected (or manually simulated for testing), we log the time, estimated impact force, and your location at that moment.

Why we collect it

Every field above exists to support one purpose: getting the right information to the right person — you, your emergency contact, or a bystander or first responder at the scene — during a real emergency. We don't use this data for advertising, analytics unrelated to safety, or any purpose beyond operating the emergency-response features described in the project.

Who can see what

  • You can always see and edit your own full profile.
  • A bystander or first responder who scans your helmet's QR code sees only a minimal emergency-triage view: your name, blood type, allergies, and emergency contact numbers. They do not see your chronic conditions, medications, or account login details, and they do not need to be logged in to see this — the whole point is that it works for a stranger at the scene.
  • Project administrators (for this prototype: the research team) can see your device status, live location while your dashboard is open, and whether a crash has been flagged, for the purpose of monitoring and demonstrating the system. Administrators do not have a separate view into your medical details beyond what's needed to operate the dashboard.
  • No one else. Your data is not shared with third parties, sold, or used for any purpose outside this project.

How it's protected

Your data is stored in a database with row-level access controls, meaning the database itself — not just the app's interface — enforces who can read which records. The public emergency lookup uses a dedicated, restricted function rather than open access to the full database. As a research prototype under active development, we cannot guarantee the same security assurances as a certified commercial medical device, and we treat any discovered issue as something to fix and disclose, not hide.

Your rights

  • You can review or correct your medical information at any time through your account settings.
  • You can request deletion of your account and all associated data by contacting Alcheon Velmonte/jamieralcheon30@gmail.com. As of this version of the prototype, deletion is a manual process handled by the research team rather than a self-service button — this is a known limitation we intend to address.
  • Providing medical information is optional, but the emergency-response features (QR-based lookup, automated alerts) will not function without at least an emergency contact and basic medical information on file.

If you are under 18

If you are a minor, we ask that a parent or guardian review this statement and be aware of your registration, since the emergency contact and medical information you provide may be accessed by strangers in an emergency situation by design.

Consent

By completing registration and medical onboarding, you confirm that:

  1. You have read and understood what data is collected and why.
  2. You consent to that data being stored and used as described above, including being shown to bystanders/first responders via QR scan in an emergency.
  3. You understand this is a research prototype, not a certified medical or safety device, and that you are participating as part of an academic research project.
