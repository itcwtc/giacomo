Engineering rationale, by subsystem

Sensing — ST LSM6DSOX

Crash detection runs on the IMU's own machine-learning core (MLC) rather than being decided in application code on the ESP32. That matters for two reasons: latency (the classification happens at the sensor, not after a round trip through the main CPU and its scheduler), and power (the ESP32 can stay in a lower-activity state while the IMU watches motion continuously, waking the main processor only when its on-chip classifier flags a likely crash).

Connectivity — SIM A7670C

The alert path deliberately does not depend on a paired phone. A cellular modem on the device itself means the crash notification goes out even if the rider's phone is destroyed, out of battery, or simply not on them — which is the scenario the whole system exists to cover.

Positioning — Quectel L76K

Multi-constellation GNSS (rather than GPS alone) trades a small amount of additional power draw for a materially faster and more reliable fix, particularly under tree cover, between buildings, or in the kind of road cuts and overpasses common on motorcycle routes.

Local record — microSD black-box logging

The seconds immediately before and after an impact are logged locally regardless of whether the cellular alert succeeds. If network conditions fail right when they're needed most, the device still preserves a record that can be recovered afterward.

Power — IP5306 PMIC

Transmitting over a cellular network draws a sharp current spike the instant the modem keys up. The power management IC exists specifically to keep the rest of the board's supply stable through that spike, so the exact moment the device needs to send its alert is never the moment it browns out.

Enclosure — IP67, abort button, piezo buzzer, RGB LED

The interface layer is built around one constraint: a crash alert that a conscious rider can't quickly cancel is a system people learn to distrust and eventually disable. The sealed abort button, audible buzzer, and status LED exist to make the countdown legible and interruptible — usable with a glove on, in the rain, by someone who may be disoriented.
