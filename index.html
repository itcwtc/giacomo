Plate no. 1 — the apparatus

GIACOMO

an emergency-response system for motorcycle riders

A helmet-mounted device that detects a crash on its own and alerts your emergency contacts with your location — without depending on your phone, or on you being conscious. Anyone who reaches you first can scan the helmet's code and see your medical details immediately, with no app and no login.

A registry is one of the oldest instruments in emergency medicine: a record kept in advance, so that a stranger can act on it without knowing you. Giacomo is that record, carried on the helmet.

LAYER I

The shell

The housing survives what the rider survives — sun, rain, road heat, constant vibration — while staying light enough that you forget it's on your helmet.

  • ASA / PETGHolds its shape in direct sun and road heat without warping.
  • FASTENERSOpens with a screwdriver, so the battery is serviceable instead of disposable.
LAYER II

The interface

Everything the rider actually touches or hears. A crash alert that can't be cancelled by a conscious rider is a system that will eventually be ignored.

  • ABORT BUTTONOne sealed press cancels a false alarm — usable with a glove on.
  • BUZZER + LEDTells you an alert is counting down, by sound and by sight.
  • MIC + SPEAKERCarries voice through the helmet when a call is connected.
LAYER III

The seal

Weatherproofing isn't a feature, it's a precondition. A device that fails in the rain fails on exactly the kind of day a rider is most likely to need it.

  • GASKETSSealed against rain, road spray and dust at every seam.
  • IP67Rated to keep working after full submersion, not just a shower.
LAYER IV

The board

One custom circuit board carries the whole system: sensing the crash, judging that it was real, finding where you are, and reaching the outside world alone.

  • ESP32-S3-1Runs the entire device independently — no phone in the loop.
  • LSM6DSOXJudges whether you've actually crashed, on the chip itself, in milliseconds.
  • SIM A7670CReaches the mobile network directly to send the alert.
  • QUECTEL L76KFixes your position using four satellite systems at once.
  • MICROSDKeeps a record of the seconds before and after impact.
LAYER V

The power

Transmitting over a mobile network draws a hard spike of current the instant it fires. This stage exists so the moment of the alert is never the moment the device gives out.

  • IP5306Holds the supply steady through the transmission spike.
  • REGULATIONClean, stable power to every chip on the board.
  • LiPo CELLRechargeable, sized to sit on a helmet without weighing it down.

Plate no. 2 — the sequence

What happens in a crash

01

Impact detected

The sensor's on-chip classifier separates an ordinary road shock from a genuine collision.

02

Grace period

A buzzer and status light count down, giving a conscious rider time to cancel a false alarm with one press.

03

Alert dispatched

If nobody cancels, the device sends your position to your emergency contacts over the mobile network — no phone involved.

04

On-scene lookup

Whoever arrives first scans the helmet's code and sees blood type, allergies and contacts in seconds.

Plate no. 3 — the scan

The stranger whofinds you first.

Most people who reach a crash aren't paramedics. They're whoever was driving behind you. They have a phone, no training, and no idea who you are — and the single most useful thing they can do is tell an ambulance your blood type before it arrives.

So the helmet carries a code anyone can scan. No app to install, no account, no permission to request. It opens to the only facts that matter in the first minutes.

NO APPOpens in the phone camera, in any browser, on any handset.
NO LOGINA bystander needs no account and no relationship to the rider.
NO EXCESSOnly triage-critical fields are shown. The rest of the record stays private.
